Vanilla Summer is the recipe I kept coming back to until it stopped being an experiment and became the way my cameras see.

Warm shadows. Soft highlights. That feeling of 4pm light, even at noon.

The [full settings are free on the recipe page](/recipes/vanilla-summer). This piece is about *why* they're set the way they are, because a recipe you understand is one you can adapt.

## The goal

I wanted summer afternoon light to be the default, not the exception.

Not orange. Not a filter. Just the gentle warmth that makes you remember a place better than it probably was.

That meant three decisions, and every setting serves one of them.

## Decision one: the shadows go warm

Most digital cameras render shadows slightly cool. Technically accurate, emotionally flat.

So the white balance is fixed at 7500K, well past daylight, with a small shift toward red and away from blue. The whole frame leans warm, and the shadows carry it most. You shouldn't notice it directly; you should just feel that the frame is friendly.

This is the heart of the recipe. If you take one idea from it, take this: white balance is where the mood lives.

## Decision two: the highlights stay soft

Hard, clipped highlights are the fastest way to make a photo look digital.

So the recipe starts from PRO Neg. Hi, runs DR400, and sets Highlight and Shadow to −1. The brights roll off early and gently, closer to how negative film gives up its highlights. Windows glow instead of burning. Skies keep their last bit of color.

The trade: you give up some punch. I found that I never missed it.

## Decision three: nothing shouts

Color sits at 0. Grain is Weak and Small, so it's there, but you have to look for it. Sharpening and Clarity stay at 0, so edges stay human.

Every setting that could call attention to itself is turned down one notch from where you'd expect. The subject of the photo should be the place, not the look.

## Loading it

The recipe targets X-Trans IV and V bodies. The [full settings are on the recipe page](/recipes/vanilla-summer), free, with a loading note. Set it to a custom slot, then forget the menus.
